**Social Media Login Data.** If you sign in with Google or GitHub, we receive your name, email address and profile picture URL from that provider, as described in "[How do we handle your social logins?](#how-do-we-handle-your-social-logins)" below.

**Videos You Upload.** When you analyze a video ad, we store the video and the settings you chose, and we process them to produce your analysis. A video may show or contain the voices of people who appear in it. You can delete an analysis and its video at any time.

**Using AdLens without an account.** If you run an analysis without signing up, we create an anonymous account for your browser: a random identifier, kept in the session cookie, with no name or email address. Your analyses and your weekly allowance are stored under it. If you later sign up or sign in in the same browser, we move them to your account and delete the anonymous one. If you don't, we delete the anonymous account and its analyses about a week after the browser last used it (see section 8).

**Sensitive Information.** We do not process sensitive information.

All personal information that you provide to us must be true, complete, and accurate, and you must notify us of any changes to such personal information.

### Information automatically collected

**_In Short:_** _Some information — such as your Internet Protocol (IP) address and/or browser and device characteristics — is collected automatically when you visit our Services._

We automatically collect certain information when you visit, use, or navigate the Services. This information does not reveal your specific identity (like your name or contact information) but may include device and usage information, such as your IP address, browser and device characteristics, operating system, language preferences, referring URLs, device name, country, location, information about how and when you use our Services, and other technical information. This information is primarily needed to maintain the security and operation of our Services, and for our internal analytics and reporting purposes.

Like many businesses, we also collect information through cookies and similar technologies.

The information we collect includes:

- _Log and Usage Data._ Log and usage data is service-related, diagnostic, usage, and performance information our servers automatically collect when you access or use our Services and which we record in log files. Depending on how you interact with us, this log data may include your IP address, device information, browser type, and settings and information about your activity in the Services (such as the date/time stamps associated with your usage, pages and files viewed, searches, and other actions you take such as which features you use), device event information (such as system activity, error reports (sometimes called "crash dumps"), and hardware settings).
- _Abuse-prevention data._ To keep our free analyses from being abused, we count the analyses started from each network: your IP address, or for IPv6 addresses the network prefix (/64) they share. We store only a keyed hash of that network, which can't be turned back into the address, with the times of those analyses, and delete it 7 days after the last analysis attempt from that network. We never store or log the IP address itself for this purpose.
- _Sign-in rate limits._ To slow down password guessing and other automated abuse, we count how often each IP address (for IPv6 addresses, the /64 network prefix) makes each kind of request to our sign-in service: signing in, signing up, other account requests, and the check, made on every page load, of whether you're signed in. We store only a keyed hash of the address together with the request's path, which can't be turned back into the address, with the count and the time counting started. Each count is deleted shortly after its time window ends, between 10 seconds and 1 hour after the first request it counts, depending on the kind of request. We never store the IP address itself for this purpose. So that nobody can flood an inbox with our emails, we also count the confirmation and password-reset emails we send to each email address, stored only as a keyed hash of the address, and delete each count an hour after the first email it counts.
